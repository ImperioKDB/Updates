## Project mission and boundaries

Investigate HTMLhost’s public platform and its generated template previews to understand observable visual design, motion, frontend architecture, responsiveness, and performance. Develop implementation-neutral, evidence-backed principles that an independent engineer can use without copying HTMLhost’s branding or proprietary code.

Always distinguish (1) the HTMLhost platform shell, (2) each public template preview and its sandboxed iframe content, and (3) published websites generated by HTMLhost. A published customer/generated site has **not** been verified in this research. Do not claim that preview behavior describes the published output. Use **VERIFIED** for directly captured evidence, **INFERRED** for well-supported but indirect conclusions, and **UNKNOWN** where evidence is insufficient. Do not attempt authentication bypass, private-customer access, publication, private APIs, or repository changes outside this research project.

### Phase 1B — motion, interaction, and diagnostic performance

Phase 1B is complete and committed. It includes five template-level reviews, a bounded REA observation-only capture, 13 Playwright/Chromium records (five desktop templates × two motion preferences, plus three phone captures), focused reversible interaction records, retained screenshots, and three original local interaction experiments. The three demos passed local Chromium smoke tests.

Important findings and limits:

- Motion differs among the five previews; the evidence does not support one shared animation library/runtime.
- Kinetica’s motion control was directly observed to synchronize its visible label, accessible name, `aria-pressed`, and sampled animation pause/resume state.
- One Creative Playground shape drag and the Business Web Studio FAQ open/close state were observed. Do not generalize one interaction to every control.
- Reduced-motion observations varied by preview. Portfolio still had two sampled preloader animations running under `reduce`; that is a follow-up question, not proof of a full-site accessibility failure.
- Futuristic Dark’s visible canvas does not prove WebGL initialized; canvas-targeted pointer delivery and its actual renderer branch remain unknown.
- Portfolio’s testimonial Next behavior differed between primary and focused captures. Business FAQ close geometry also differed across captures. These contradictions are preserved, not silently resolved.
- The matrix is a one-run diagnostic, **not** a cross-template performance ranking. `body-visible` timing includes host/stabilization waits; Resource Timing may omit cross-origin bytes; LCP/CLS entries are provisional; image counters are early snapshots; and RAF callback cadence is not FPS. Lighthouse was unavailable; no real-device trace, CPU/GPU profile, field Web Vitals, or repeated-run statistics were collected.
- At 390 px, Creative Playground’s preview document reported 1041 px scroll width; this is a likely overflow condition to inspect, not a design pattern to copy.

No private site or authenticated workflow was used, and no generated customer site was verified.

## Recommended next work: a bounded Phase 1B closure pass

The highest-value immediate follow-up is to resolve the known contradictions without recapturing the corpus:

1. **Portfolio testimonial:** use a fresh public preview context, identify the active slide and exact next-control element, record its accessible/DOM state before the click, dispatch a single normal user click, wait a fixed interval longer than the measured transition, and record the resulting active slide plus relevant classes/opacity/transform. Repeat only enough times to determine whether the sequence is deterministic. Check console/page errors. If outcome still varies, label it unresolved.
2. **Business FAQ close:** record the controlled panel’s computed grid rows/height and `aria-expanded` immediately after close and again after a fixed settling interval longer than 500 ms. Separate transition-time measurement from final state.
3. **Futuristic Dark canvas:** inspect the public runtime without probing hidden/private endpoints. Verify whether a WebGL context exists or a fallback is visible, and whether pointer events reach the canvas handler. If safe and reproducible, capture only this target’s event path and before/after state. Do not equate ancestor-level pointer events or identical screenshots with proof that canvas input works.
4. Keep this pass small, use the existing interaction harness where appropriate, and add a dated append-only follow-up record plus a short note to the motion audit/master report. Do not overwrite raw Phase 1B data.

A separate future performance study may be valuable, but it needs explicit scope, repeat runs, a browser performance trace and/or Lighthouse installation, and ideally real-device testing. Do not manufacture missing metrics or turn the existing diagnostic table into rankings. After the closure pass, report remaining unknowns and stop for review unless the user asks to proceed further.
